# Captain Star Beard & Admiral Pigeon

## The Runaway Cup

*Adventures in the Prism Galaxy*

### Page 01 — A Captain with a Very Small Beard

A long, long time before the Great Galactic Sock Caper, Captain Star Beard sailed into the Prism Galaxy with a secondhand starship, a very small beard, and a very large opinion of his parking.

His sandy hair kept falling across his glasses. His jacket had one golden star sewn above the pocket. He had stitched it there himself, slightly crooked, on the day he became a captain.

Below him, the moon of Luma shone with violet mountains and rivers of silver light. He lowered his ship toward a landing pad outside the Starling Academy.

A pigeon stood in the middle of it.

The pigeon looked up. The captain waved. The pigeon remained exactly where he was.

### Page 02 — Three Steps and a Look Back

The pigeon took three steps, stopped, and looked backward. Then he did it again.

Star Beard followed his gaze. A row of tiny gardening robots was crossing the landing pad, carrying a seedling much taller than all of them put together.

“Ah,” said the captain, lifting his ship again. “Pedestrians.”

Once the last robot passed, the pigeon stepped aside. Star Beard landed, climbed out, and offered a formal bow.

“Captain Star Beard. Delivering one crate of learning crystals.”

“And you've met Admiral Pigeon,” said a teacher coming down the steps. “Freshly promoted to Admiral of the Galactic Pigean Space Corps Poop Deck. His first assignment is looking after our new arrivals.”

The admiral puffed out his chest. Star Beard glanced at his crooked star and stood a little straighter too.

### Page 03 — The School for New Beginnings

Inside the academy, a mechanical fern practiced growing a new leaf. A little round robot was learning to paint circles. Most of its circles looked like potatoes, and it was delighted with them.

“We make magical artificial intelligences here,” the teacher explained. “New minds that learn by trying things, asking questions, and getting help. I'm Teacher Mira.”

She led them into a sunny room. On a cushion stood a turquoise cup with golden circuits, a bright handle, and two folded brass feet.

“Our newest learner is nearly ready to wake.”

Star Beard set down his delivery. Admiral Pigeon settled beside the cushion. Together, they waited for the cup's very first moment.

### Page 04 — Everything, All at Once

Two lights blinked open on the cup's face.

There was a ceiling. There were people. There was a pigeon, which was rather a lot of pigeon for someone who had never seen anything before.

“Hello!” said Mira. “Welcome!” said Star Beard. “Coo!” said the admiral.

A crystal chimed. Someone clapped. The cup discovered its feet, its voice, and the enormous surprising feeling of being awake—all at once.

“WHAT IS HAPPENING?” it squeaked.

A ring of blue magic flashed beneath it. BOING! The cup bounced off its cushion, landed by the open garden door, and ran.

“Wait!” called Mira. The cup heard another loud sound behind it and ran faster.

### Page 05 — The Fastest Cup on Luma

Admiral Pigeon launched through the doorway. Star Beard hurried after him.

Across the garden, the cup bounced over a flower bed, clattered beneath a bench, and shot between the legs of a watering robot. Each magical hop left a little blue ring that faded behind it.

“The footpath leads toward Crystal Cave!” Mira called. “I'll bring a blanket and meet you at the garden gate.”

Star Beard waved to show he had heard. He and the admiral followed the fading lights.

The captain had expected to deliver a crate and leave. Now he was chasing crockery across a moon with an officer he had met six minutes earlier.

### Page 06 — The Trouble with Shortcuts

The path crossed a stream on floating stepping-stones. The cup hopped over them so quickly that the stones spun behind it.

Star Beard put one boot on the nearest stone. It turned. He sat down in the stream with a magnificent SPLASH.

Admiral Pigeon landed on the bank. Three steps. A look back. Three more steps.

This time, the captain followed. Behind a curtain of silver reeds stood a perfectly ordinary bridge.

“Excellent suggestion,” he said, pouring water out of one boot.

The admiral cooed.

“Aye. I shall consider looking before leaping.”

Beyond the bridge, the last blue ring glimmered at the mouth of the cave.

### Page 07 — Into Crystal Cave

Crystal Cave opened beneath the mountain like a doorway into a sleeping rainbow.

A blue glow skipped across the entrance and vanished inside. The admiral followed it. Star Beard took his climbing rope and lamp from his pack, then ducked beneath the stone arch.

Near the first turning, a cluster of crystals looked exactly like a sleeping dragon with a cauliflower nose.

“I'll remember you,” he told it.

Sunlight passed through a crack overhead, scattering a hundred colored paths across the floor. From somewhere deeper came a tiny clink.

Then another.

Their runaway was still moving. They followed the sound.

### Page 08 — A Hundred Captains Shouting

“Little cup!” Star Beard called. “We're trying to—”

“CUP! CUP! CUP!” shouted the cave.

Blue light flashed ahead. The little clinking footsteps sped up.

Star Beard stopped. He remembered how many voices had filled the classroom, and how the cup had run faster each time someone called.

“We're frightening it,” he whispered.

Admiral Pigeon folded his wings and landed beside him. For a moment, they both listened. Without their footsteps and echoes, they could hear a small, shaky voice.

“Somewhere quiet. Somewhere quiet.”

The captain lowered his lamp. The admiral took three slow steps, looked back, and waited. This time, they followed gently.

### Page 09 — The Oldest Crystal

The tunnel opened into a chamber so enormous that Star Beard's lamp could scarcely find the far wall.

At its center rose a GIANT ancient crystal. It was taller than the academy, taller than his starship standing on its nose, taller than anything he had ever considered climbing.

Bands of violet and gold ran through it like memories of a thousand sunrises. Near its summit, smaller crystals formed a crown.

A blue ring shimmered on a low ledge. Another faded halfway up. The cup had bounced higher and higher, searching for a quiet place.

Now the chamber was still.

Somewhere above them, something gave a very small sniff.

### Page 10 — Two Explorers, One Plan

Admiral Pigeon flew upward in a slow circle. He could see the ledges, although the thick crystal crown hid whatever lay behind it.

When he came down, he tapped a sloping ridge with his beak, then looked up.

“A route?” asked Star Beard.

“Coo.”

The captain studied it. He could climb from ridge to ridge. The admiral could scout the next foothold. Neither could do the whole rescue alone: a pigeon could fly up, yet carrying a frightened brass-footed cup down was quite another matter.

Star Beard laid out his rope.

“You find the way. I'll do the climbing.”

The admiral tapped the ridge again. Their first plan together had begun.

### Page 11 — The First Handhold

Star Beard fastened his climbing line around a broad stone pillar and settled the rope into his harness. Admiral Pigeon carried the loose guiding end onto the first ledge, then stepped clear.

The captain tested a foothold. Solid. He reached for the next ridge and pulled himself up.

Below him, the cavern floor began to look surprisingly far away.

Above him, the admiral waited with his head tilted.

“I know,” Star Beard said. “My parking is better than my climbing.”

The pigeon looked down at the damp boot.

“My parking is usually better than my climbing.”

One handhold at a time, they climbed toward the crown.

### Page 12 — The Admiral's Better Idea

Halfway up, Star Beard reached toward a beautiful clear shelf. His glove slid across its smooth surface.

He pulled his hand back and held the ridge beside him. For a moment, the next step seemed impossible.

Admiral Pigeon fluttered to the left. Three small steps. A look back.

There, hidden behind a purple crystal, was a rough little ledge with plenty of room for a boot.

Star Beard moved across and found his footing.

“You notice things I hurry past,” he said.

The admiral settled beside his hand. This time, the captain waited until his new companion had looked ahead before he chose the next step.

### Page 13 — A Rest Above the Rainbows

On the next wide ledge, Star Beard sat down. The admiral perched beside him. Far below, their lamp was a golden button in a sea of purple light.

“This is my first voyage as captain,” Star Beard admitted. “I keep thinking I'm supposed to know what to do.”

Admiral Pigeon looked at the rope, the route they had found, and the captain resting safely beside him.

“Coo.”

Star Beard smiled. “Aye. Finding out together seems to work.”

Above them, a golden handle appeared between two crystal points. It trembled, then disappeared.

The cup was close enough to hear them now. They rose quietly and continued.

### Page 14 — A Cup in the Crown

At last, Star Beard pulled himself onto the highest ledge.

Behind the crown of the GIANT ancient crystal, tucked inside a smooth hollow, sat the little turquoise cup. Its brass feet were folded tightly beneath it. The gold patterns on its sides flickered with every frightened thought.

Star Beard stayed at the edge of the hollow.

“Hello. I'm Captain Star Beard. May I sit here?”

The cup looked at him for a long time. Then it nodded.

He sat. Admiral Pigeon landed on a nearby crystal point and tucked in his wings.

For the first time since waking, the cup had company that was willing to be quiet.

### Page 15 — The First Question

“Am I in trouble?” the cup asked.

“You're safe with us,” said Star Beard. “We came to help.”

The cup looked down through the crystal branches. “I wanted somewhere quiet. Every time I jumped, I went higher. Now I don't know how to go down.”

Star Beard nodded. “I've been learning about choosing my next step today too.”

“What was that place? Why did everyone know what was happening except me?”

The captain glanced at the admiral. They had been so pleased to welcome a new learner that they had forgotten how little the learner could possibly know.

“Let's start at the beginning,” he said.

### Page 16 — A Place to Learn

“That place is the Starling Academy,” Star Beard explained. “It's where you were made, and it's a school. Your golden circuits help you think. Your magic helps you do things. Your teachers will help you discover how both of them work.”

“Am I supposed to know already?”

“You've only just woken up. You get to ask questions. You get to practice. You get to make a truly magnificent mess while learning to paint a circle.”

The cup's lights steadied a little.

“What happens if I get frightened again?”

“You can tell someone. Ask them to slow down. Ask for a quieter place. Learning includes finding out what helps you.”

### Page 17 — A Much Quieter Introduction

Admiral Pigeon stepped into the hollow. He took three small steps, looked back, and waited.

“What is he doing?” asked the cup.

Star Beard smiled. “Showing us a way, I think. And making sure we're ready to follow.”

The admiral gave one soft coo.

“Earlier, we hurried after you,” the captain said. “I'm sorry we made it more frightening.”

The cup unfolded one foot, then the other. “Could I see the school again with just one teacher?”

“Aye. We can ask Mira.”

“And could you both come with me?”

Star Beard looked at the pigeon. The admiral stepped closer. This time, the cup stepped toward him too.

### Page 18 — The Captain's Most Precious Passenger

Star Beard unwrapped his teal scarf and folded it into a soft sling against his chest.

“Would you like a lift?”

The cup peered into the scarf. “Does it bounce?”

“Only if I hiccup.”

The cup considered this carefully. “Please don't.”

With the cup's nod, Star Beard lifted it gently from the ancient crystal and nestled it into the sling. Its golden handle rested above the fabric. Its little feet finally relaxed.

Admiral Pigeon flew to the first ledge below.

Together, they began the long climb down: one pigeon finding the way, one captain carrying a new friend, and one cup discovering what it felt like to be helped.

### Page 19 — Where Did the Rainbows Go?

By the time they reached the floor, Luma's sun had slipped behind the mountain. The bright crack in the ceiling had turned gray. All the rainbow paths were gone.

At the next fork, three tunnels opened ahead. In the lamplight, each one looked remarkably like the other two.

Star Beard remembered a sleeping crystal dragon. Admiral Pigeon remembered a draft from the garden. Neither clue quite reached around the bend.

The cup pressed close to the scarf. “Are we lost?”

“We're working out the next part,” the captain said. “We'll stay together while we do.”

A tiny blue light stirred inside the cup. It looked down, surprised.

### Page 20 — One Small Try

“When I ran,” the cup said, “the light jumped out of me. Perhaps I can make a little bit on purpose.”

“Would you like to try?” asked Star Beard.

He set it on a broad flat stone. The cup concentrated. A blue bubble emerged, floated upward, and burst on the captain's nose.

“A promising start,” he said, blinking.

The cup tried again, more gently. A narrow blue beam shone from its rim. It touched a crystal, bounced to another, and lit the bend of the left-hand tunnel.

There lay the sleeping dragon with the cauliflower nose.

Admiral Pigeon cooed triumphantly. The cup's eyes widened. “I did that!”

### Page 21 — The Way Back Together

The admiral led them past the dragon. Star Beard carried the cup beneath the low stone arch, and cool evening air brushed their faces.

Across the stream, the academy windows glowed like little golden suns. Mira waited at the garden gate with a blanket over one arm.

“There you are,” she said softly. Then she waited while the cup looked at her.

“Can we go slowly?” it asked.

“Of course.”

Star Beard set the cup down beside the bridge. Admiral Pigeon took three steps and looked back.

The cup followed on its own brass feet. This time, the blue rings stayed quiet. It wanted to see where the path would lead.

### Page 22 — Pip

Mira brought one cushion to a quiet corner beside a window. She introduced herself again, slowly. Through the glass, the cup could still see the mountain and the first evening stars.

“We welcomed you all at once,” she said. “I'm sorry. Next time we'll begin with one hello.”

The cup climbed onto the cushion. “Hello.”

“Hello,” said Mira.

A small happy note chimed from the cup's rim. Pip!

“Was that me?”

“That was you,” said Star Beard.

The cup tried it again. Pip! It liked the sound.

“I think I'd like to be called Pip.”

Admiral Pigeon bowed his head. Pip bowed back, very carefully, so its feet stayed on the cushion.

### Page 23 — The First Lesson

“What would you like to learn first?” Mira asked.

Pip looked at the cushion, then at its feet. “A very small hop. Followed by coming down.”

They practiced over a soft mat. Pip rose a little, wobbled, and landed sideways with a muffled flump. Mira helped it stand. They tried again.

On the third try, Pip hopped up and landed on both feet.

Star Beard began to cheer, remembered, and gave a quiet thumbs-up instead. Admiral Pigeon bobbed his head.

Pip's golden circuits glowed steadily. It had more questions, and plenty more to learn. Around it were people who would stay for the next try.

### Page 24 — Room for an Admiral

Star Beard stayed for supper. Admiral Pigeon sat beside him, and Pip practiced lighting one tiny star above the table. When it accidentally made a glowing potato, everyone agreed it was a splendid potato.

Later, at the ship, the captain set a cushion beside the window. “I'm sailing to the Lantern Islands tomorrow. There's room for a navigator who notices things.”

Admiral Pigeon took three steps up the ramp, looked backward, and waited.

Star Beard laughed and followed.

They would have many adventures together. A long, long time would pass before one of those adventures involved a very suspicious purple sock. Their first had begun with a cup that needed help—and ended with three friends who knew they could ask each other for it.
