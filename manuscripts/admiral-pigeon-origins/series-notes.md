# Before He Was Admiral Pigeon

## A trilogy for Juliet

Three connected chapter books about the small pigeon who grew up in a rock wall, joined the Planetary Pigeon Airforce, transferred into the Galactic Pigean Space Corps, and earned the extraordinarily serious appointment of **Admiral of the Galactic Pigean Space Corps Poop Deck**. The third book's shorter title is **Admiral of the Pigeon's Poop Deck**.

These are reading manuscripts. Each volume has a complete adventure and an ending, with characters, consequences, and discoveries continuing into the next. Illustrated library editions can follow the manuscripts without compressing their stories into summaries.

## Reading order

1. **The Little Pigeon in the Great Stone Wall** — A family history becomes a young pigeon's own decision to serve. His first great adventure is a rescue through the storm passages of his home planet.
2. **The Pigeon Beyond the Sky** — An experienced planetary flyer becomes a beginner in space, finds his crew, and discovers what the poop deck can do during a dangerous convoy rescue.
3. **Admiral of the Pigeon's Poop Deck** — A seasoned commander and his fellow pigeons help turn the Great War of the Bellflower Galaxy through courage, observation, and a spectacularly inconvenient attack on the enemy's view.

## Shared story foundation

### Pigeon and his family

- The hero is called **Pigeon** throughout. He is an ordinary small gray pigeon with an iridescent turquoise-and-violet neck. His bearing grows into the familiar chest-puffing admiral; he wears no clothes or hat. Service insignia belong on perch plates and cabin doors. Equipment belongs to the vehicle or the job.
- Pigeons converse in their own coos. The narrator renders those conversations as dialogue for the reader. This does not establish human speech: humans in the later Star Beard story hear his coos and interpret his posture.
- His home is a small, comfortable family nook in the rock wall of **Old Wing Cavern**, an ancient warrior-pigeon cave on **Pebble**, in the **Bellflower Galaxy**. The family home is called **the little home in the wall**. It contains ordinary bowls, crumbs, a crooked shelf, and a window opening into the cavern.
- **Mother Tansy** repairs and makes practical things. **Father Bram** tends the cavern's rooftop seed gardens. **Gran Cora** is a retired military messenger and the keeper of family stories. All three remain alive throughout the trilogy.
- An old family **route cloth** records shelter ledges, air passages, and places to wait together. It is a useful inherited map, not a prophecy, secret weapon, or magical answer to every problem. An old ancestor, **Messenger Fern**, is remembered for returning to guide stranded flyers during a storm.
- The familiar gesture develops naturally: **three steps, stop, look back**. It means checking that somebody is ready and can follow. Its first important use is during Book One's rescue. Its full shape should recur at meaningful moments, not on every page.

### Recurring crew

- **Bramble**: sturdy pigeon, excellent load handler and practical improviser, dry humor. Meets Pigeon in Airforce training in Book One. Transfers with him in Book Two. Commands a rescue formation in Book Three.
- **Tiller**: quick pigeon, initially treats flying as a competition, later a trusted scout. Meets Pigeon in Book One. Transfers with him in Book Two. Leads scouts in Book Three. Rivalry resolves through earned cooperation rather than humiliation.
- **Sergeant Flint**: demanding, observant Airforce instructor, introduced in Book One; insists on checking the flock before departure. May send a letter or appear at the final ceremony; stays with the planetary service.
- **Dr. Nib**: space-corps engineer, a small pigeon with an untidy laboratory and delight in precise, ridiculous discoveries. Introduced in Book Two; continues in Book Three.
- **Captain Vela**: capable senior pigeon officer commanding the space-corps carrier **Homeward**. Introduced in Book Two; remains an active allied commander in Book Three. Helps others develop judgment rather than supplying every answer.

### Progression and chronology

- Book One begins in childhood, gives room for family discovery and the decision to enlist, then explicitly moves forward to enlistment age before training. End with Pigeon earning his place as a qualified Airforce flyer after the storm rescue. A wider horizon can be visible without transferring him yet.
- Book Two begins after several seasons of planetary service. Pigeon, Bramble, and Tiller transfer to the **Galactic Pigean Space Corps**. Pigeon must learn rather than rely on his past success. A smaller rescue involving an Iron Talon blockade ends with him receiving command of a patrol and the rank **Flight Lieutenant**.
- Book Three begins **years later**, after further service and promotions. **Commander Pigeon** already leads several pigeon flights. His experience exists before the climactic battle. After the decisive battle he is awarded the rank of **Admiral**, with the full appointment **Admiral of the Galactic Pigean Space Corps Poop Deck**, commanding the fleet's pigeon operations. Earned honor and absurd wording both matter.
- Pigeon is a **brand-new admiral** when he meets Star Beard in the next story. Keep the homecoming brief and the academy appointment his **first assignment as admiral**, following directly from the trilogy's award. The formal spelling **Pigean** reflects Roshi's subsequent naming instruction for the connected story.
- The trilogy precedes **The Runaway Cup**, itself a long, long time before **The Great Galactic Sock Caper**. No numbered year or exact age is needed.

### Space travel, poop, and the war

- Space pigeons travel outside ships in small, transparent **flight bubbles**: ship-launched capsules with breathable air, room for wings, and steering by perch pedals and wing movements. They can move in vacuum because the capsule's little thrusters move them. Wings alone do not propel an unprotected bird through vacuum. These are vehicles, not clothes.
- The **poop deck** is the upper rear flight deck of a pigeon carrier. Its ordinary name is delightfully literal in pigeon service. Bubbles launch from this deck; the crew's droppings are collected there for the experimental **splat pods** introduced in Book Two.
- **Splat pods** contain pigeon poop and Nib's fictional stardew gel. The gel lets the mixture spread across exterior observation lenses in space. Pigeons aim and release the pods from their bubbles. The ingredients remain fantastical; no real fabrication instructions.
- The attack smears viewing lenses and aiming eyes. It does not damage engines, turn off all equipment, instantly defeat a fleet, or harm a crew through the glass. Enemy crews retain instruments, radio, shutters, and cleaning systems. They can adapt; allies must do more than repeat the same attack.
- The aggressor is **Marshal Clang's Iron Talon Fleet**, which seizes shared travel gates and blocks food and supplies to make worlds submit. Its ships have conspicuously polished observation lenses. Its crews are living individuals; surrender and rescue are possible. Avoid blanket claims about an inherently evil species.
- Book One's main danger is a planetary storm, independent of the later war. Book Two involves a local blockade and a successful convoy rescue, exposing the expanding conflict without ending it. Book Three covers the **Great War of the Bellflower Galaxy**, culminating at **Bellflower Gate**, where the enemy tries to close the civilian evacuation route.
- The pigeon formations are pivotal under Pigeon's command. Their tactics open a route for allied ships to evacuate civilians and outmaneuver Clang; the pigeons do not win every part of the war alone. The first splat attack is countered. The final plan requires the established map-reading habit, distributed leadership, Bramble's rescue work, Tiller's scouting, and Nib's engineering.
- Keep peril vivid and battles exuberant, readable, and non-graphic. Courage involves a real cost or difficult choice. Include joy, ambition, pride in service, and glorious pigeon absurdity alongside care for others. Let actions carry the meaning instead of ending every scene with a lesson.

### Ending at the next story's doorstep

Book Three resolves the battle, the award, and a return to the little family home before its short final coda. The new assignment is to **Starling Academy**, on the moon **Luma** in the **Prism Galaxy**, to look after new arrivals and oversee preparations for a magical artificial intelligence in a turquoise cup. The cup is **still being built**, with gold circuit filigree, one golden handle, two brass legs, and no arms.

The cup has no name yet and does not wake in this trilogy. In **The Runaway Cup**, it wakes, becomes frightened, is helped home, and only then chooses **Pip** as its name. **Teacher Mira** can sign the assignment letter. Pigeon and young **Captain Star Beard** have not met. Their first meeting, the captain's first voyage in his unnamed secondhand blue-and-brass ship, and their joint cup rescue belong to that following book. Keep those discoveries intact.

The active owner of **The Runaway Cup** confirmed the cup's appearance, naming sequence, academy setting, first-meeting chronology, and Pigeon's ordinary uncostumed appearance while these manuscripts were being written.

### Details established in the manuscripts

- **Book One:** High Perch Station is the training base and Pigeon's first regular posting. Lantern Ledge is the storm-struck settlement; Keeper Moss and young Button are among the fourteen rescued residents. Bell Hollow is an echoing passage on the route. The family cloth remains intact and receives the new route observations. Mother's repair supports the home's shelf while leaving its familiar crooked shape.
- **Book Two:** The original cloth travels aboard *Homeward* in a tube repaired by Mother Tansy. Pigeon uses its plain reverse for new learning maps and copies drawings into his letters; he keeps the cloth itself. Commander Grint leads the local Iron Talon blockade at Larkspur Passage. The Hearth Convoy consists of *Apricot*, *Kettle*, and *Lantern*, bound for Mallow Station. Captain Hobb commands *Kettle*. The young passenger with a paper bird is a fieldmouse; she gives Pigeon the bird after the rescue, and he keeps it beside the cloth and his letters. The rescued convoy reaches Mallow; the blockade remains at the end of this volume. Pigeon's new command is *Homeward*'s passage patrol.
- **Book Three:** Lieutenant Rusk is a moth in the enemy service, a separate character from Commander Grint. Captain Marr, a fieldmouse, commands the civilian *Teacup*. Clang's flagship is the *Crown of Order*. The Bellhouse controls Bellflower Gate. The former freight circuit supplies small-flight staging and signals; it does not provide a secret tunnel through which the civilian convoy escapes.
