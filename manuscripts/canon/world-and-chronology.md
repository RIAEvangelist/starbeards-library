# Shared world and chronology

This editorial guide joins the ten existing stories without inventing a calendar, renaming ships or turning every illustration difference into a new event. Read it with the [character records](README.md) and [source inventory](coverage.md). Source prose, series notes and later direct author corrections establish events; new profile backstories extend that world under their own visible label.

The Lantern Islands are a proposed next destination at Runaway Cup's ending; that voyage is not narrated here.

## Character and visual continuity

Pigeon remains a natural gray pigeon throughout childhood, service and command: two wings, two feet, charcoal beak, pale cere, turquoise/violet neck iridescence, pink or red feet. Clothes, hats, human hands and body-worn rank insignia contradict the established design. Perch plates and cabin doors carry titles. Coos can be interpreted as dialogue for readers; human speech is not a new biological ability. His mother Tansy, father Bram and grandmother Cora remain alive. Messenger Fern is an earlier ancestor. Her route cloth preserves practical experience, not prophecy.

Star Beard and Starbeard are the captain's source-era spellings. The prequel explicitly links him to the later sock adventure. Moonlit's Admiral title and benevolent-pirate presentation coexist with Pluto's explicit description of a captain who is not a pirate. Keep the wording appropriate to each story; no invented promotion resolves the variation. Doughnut's sash, coat, hat and beard sparks are an outfit and confidence progression, not a replacement first commission. Juliet's age and family relationship to him remain unspecified. Uni keeps hooves, a white body and golden horn; rainbow and purple mane descriptions remain tied to their stories.

Pip has turquoise porcelain, gold circuitry, one handle, two brass feet and no arms. The cup wakes on Runaway Cup page 4 and names itself on page 22. Earlier uses of “Pip” in production prompts are design shorthand. The feet stay folded in the summit conversation until page 17. The captain's lamp remains on the cavern floor during the ascent; the scarf becomes a front-of-chest sling from page 18. A rear view must not turn that sling into back carrying.

The three Melodians retain their existing round, furry, antennaed design. **New editorial continuity:** the golden individual is the eldest; the blue and lavender individuals receive separate backstories. Prose establishes the eldest and trio, while the color-to-role assignment is newly adopted here. Later fairy-like art does not replace this cast. The First Flower keeps its inner petal and gives the missing note in response to the crew's freely offered song.

Sensei, the Lumi elder and the cosmic elder remain three separate figures. Pluto's little companion is an unnamed tiny animal; its illustrated teal fur does not establish a species or connect it to Pip. Captain Marr is the adult fieldmouse captain of Teacup; Lantern's young paper-bird passenger is a separate individual. Similar unnamed captains, keepers and neighbors remain distinct or explicitly unresolved when the text gives no link.

## Magic, technology and story limits

- Uni's horn assists with light, warmth and magic; shared observation, effort and listening still matter. It does not open Moonlit's musical door by force.
- Golden dream seed, humming seed and three-petaled porch starflower are separate things. Naptune's moon is awake before the dream seed returns. The starflower has exactly three petals even where an older image differs.
- The family cloth records routes, and current weather knowledge can correct an old route. It never guarantees rescue by itself.
- Space flight bubbles provide air and thrusters. Nib's fictional splat pods combine pigeon poop and stardew gel to obscure external viewing lenses temporarily. They do not disable engines or navigation instruments. Opponents adapt, and the convoy rescue does not instantly end the war.
- Pip's golden circuits help it think, and magic helps it act. Fear causes early uncontrolled hopping; practiced control emerges gradually. Being an artificial intelligence does not remove its need for patient teaching.
- The Heart of the Isles is a glowing sphere in Pluto's manuscript; older heart-shaped artwork does not change that description. Memory imagery should depict the established journey rather than add undocumented lion, fairy or dragon encounters.
- The cosmic elder's tiny stars are gifts within the shared dream; waking powers are unestablished.
- The Sock Thief is reported in conversation and absent from the present investigation. “Static Cling” is a joke. A missing sock does not establish an actual new theft.

## Editorial additions and exclusions

Every profile's **Beyond the pages** section is newly authored fiction. It may supply habits, earlier experiences and small continuing activities consistent with the story. It is not evidence that an older book already contains those scenes. Descriptive identities stay descriptive; an unnamed person receives no invented source name. Communities have collective backstories while their established section preserves distinct members and uncertain overlaps.

Inanimate tools and landmarks stay outside the cast: the route cloth, ships, folded paper bird, “Bed Admiral” stuffed squid and Runaway Cup's dragon-shaped rock are not newly sentient beings. Living flowers, seeds, singing trees and personified moons do belong in the guide. Artwork-only nestmates are explicitly identified as visual evidence.

The ten-story review establishes no Moon Whale, Rosa, Fergus, Cookie, Bosun or Briarwind as a named participant in these books. New stories can introduce new characters; a comic adaptation cannot claim such an invention was already in its source. Full original prose and its humor remain the adaptation authority. Private photographic references and real family biography remain outside the public fictional canon.
